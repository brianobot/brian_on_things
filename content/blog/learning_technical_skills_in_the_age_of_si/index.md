## Introduction
Like many technical or skilled persons in the world at the moment, it's becoming increasingly hard to feel special about the technical skill you possess, especially a skill like a software mastery or software engineering. In recent times, I have face to face with the reality that what made me special (with the hands in the air defensively) in terms of my technical skills and 
experience in software programming is largely not special anymore.

Now I have a real issue, Why should I invest real time like was done in the past, to gain supreme mastery of topics that now feels very euphmeral, Why should I commit my weekend to understanding how Python's dictionary actually works under the hood, and care about the details linked to open addressing, when I can generate more features far beyond my controls in a product others could just as well vibe code?

At my most recent workplace (<small>sad memories</small>), apart from the issues linked with lack of proper management and lack of a concrete vision for the company's moat, I basically clocked out at some point, and just became a feature sprouting organism, attending demos after demos to wow *always almost-at-the-verge-of-paying-clients*. This was sad for me, because outside making a living with the skills I had acquired, I do genuinely love solving problems with the tools in my arsenal.

## What was it really about for you?
This answer to this question would probably justifies most people's stance on whether to bother commiting to learning certain technical skills, if it was solely about implementing a feature, I guess you're better off moving fast and generating one with an LLM model or some agent of sort, but if it was really about mastering systems, understanding principles and unconciously or conciously transferring those skills to other unrelated domains then the fear of being replaced should be less (notice how I said less, it doesn't go away). This does not invalidate the fear of losing out on a race of doing so much more with limited time and capital compared to any other time in history.

## My Personal initial motivation
For me, it was always about mastery, I mean that mastery made my work easier, but I always loved the bits and bytes of it all, the quirks you had to get use to, and understand why there were, the rationales, the processes, it made commiting to mastery very rewarding. All these contributed to buildings ones problem solving muscles across domains. For example I do this thing in my real life activities, where I try to do alot of things concurrently in a beautifully scheduled way, mimicing asynchronouse programming style: Like how I try to hack my chores and wait times on other activity by using those wait times to attend to other pending activities and I do it conciously and get excitement from being my own Asynchronous runtime.

## Is History Repetiting itself?
I am sure a certain sect of skilled person experienced things like this in the past, take for example, In the chess world, computers have practically perfected playing chess, and have since surpassed humans, but people still study it, and deep understanding seperates good players from great ones. And that mastery is not any less valuable because it's beneath the level of a computers'.

I think the same argument can be made for what is happening to software engineering and maths at this point in history, ofcourse everyone can spin up a React App and get a well documented API powering it, but I believe it's a matter of time, before the mass hysteria dies
and people get bored of the AI hype. Is there a place of SI in work, ofcourse, but I have accepted that mastery is personal and it does infact reward more even outside of the specific domain of application.

## Conclusion
I could be terribly wrong, man! I don't know the future, but this sounds quite reasonable, so if you're reading this from some distant day in the future and you laugh at my shear misdirection on how things were going to go, I want you to know I too was open to the possibility that things happened in ways I could not have predicted. Soooo!! Do whatever makes you happy, and if that includes mastery of the internals of Fortran or memorizing factorials of a very large number, by all means go crazy.
