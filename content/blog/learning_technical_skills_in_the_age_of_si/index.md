## Introduction
Like many technical or skilled persons in the world at the moment, in the age of SI[^si] it's becoming increasingly hard to feel special about the technical skill you possess, especially a skill like software mastery or software engineering. In recent times, I have had to come face to face with the reality that what made me special (with the hands in the air, defensively), in terms of my technical skills and 
experience in software programming, is largely not special anymore.

 [^si]: SI: Super Intelligence - President Donald Trump signed an Executive Order on September 29, 2026, officially directing federal agencies to replace the terms "Artificial Intelligence" (AI) and "AI" with "Super Intelligence" (SI) in all official correspondence, public communications, and non-statutory documents. [Read more](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/09/inaugurating-the-era-of-super-intelligence/)

Now I have a real issue: Why should I invest real time like was done in the past, to gain mastery of topics that now feels very ephemeral? Why should I commit my weekend to understanding how Python's dictionary actually works under the hood and care about the details linked to open addressing, when I can generate more features far beyond my control in a product others could just as well vibe code?

At my most recent workplace (<small>sad memories of my employed self</small>), apart from the issues linked with lack of a concrete vision for the company's moat, I basically clocked out at some point, and just became a feature sprouting organism, attending demo after demo to wow *always almost-at-the-verge-of-paying-clients*. This was sad for me, because outside making a living with the skills I had acquired, I do genuinely love solving problems with the tools in my arsenal that actually ended up being used by people.

## What was it really about for you?
The answer to this question would probably justify most people's stance on whether to bother committing to learning certain technical skills, if it was solely about implementing a feature, I guess you're better off moving fast and generating one with an LLM or some agent of sorts, but if it was really about mastering systems, understanding principles and unconsciously or consciously transferring those skills to other unrelated domains then the fear of being replaced should be less (notice how I said less, it doesn't go away). This does not invalidate the fear of losing out on a race of doing so much more with limited time and capital compared to any other time in history.

## My Personal initial motivation
For me, it was always about mastery, I mean that mastery made my work easier, but I always loved the bits and bytes of it all, the quirks you had to get used to, and understand why they were, the rationales, the processes, it made committing to mastery very rewarding. All these contributed to building one's problem solving muscles across domains. For example I do this thing in my real life activities, where I try to do a lot of things concurrently in a beautifully scheduled way, mimicking asynchronous programming style: Like how I try to hack my chores and wait times on other activity by using those wait times to attend to other pending activities and I do it consciously and get excitement from being my own asynchronous runtime.

## Is History Repeating itself?
I am sure a certain set of skilled person experienced things like this in the past, take for example, In the chess world, computers have practically perfected playing chess, and have since surpassed humans, but people still study it, and deep understanding separates good players from great ones. And that mastery is not any less valuable because it's beneath a computer's.

I think the same argument can be made for what is happening to software engineering and maths at this point in history, of course everyone can spin up a React App and get a well documented API powering it, but I believe it's a matter of time, before the mass hysteria dies off (this is actual wishful thinking)
and people get bored of the AI hype. Is there a place of SI in work? of course, but I have accepted that mastery is personal and it does in fact reward more even outside of the specific domain of application.

## Conclusion
I could be terribly wrong, man! I don't know the future, but this sounds quite reasonable, so if you're reading this from some distant day in the future and you laugh at my sheer misdirection on how things were going to go, I want you to know I too was open to the possibility that things happened in ways I could not have predicted. Soooo!! Do whatever makes you happy, and if that includes mastery of the internals of Fortran or memorizing factorials of a very large number, by all means go crazy.
