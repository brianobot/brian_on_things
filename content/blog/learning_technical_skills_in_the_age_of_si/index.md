## Introduction
Like many technical or skilled persons in the world at the moment, I at some point in recent times have come face to face with the 
reality that what sort of made me special (so to say) in terms of my technical skills and problem solving ability in terms of creativity, 
design, and overall acquired systematic approach to solving certain categories of issues especially with software and programming was largely not special anymore.

The main issue being a real dismotivation to invest real time like was done in the past to gain supreme mastery of topics that now felt very euphmeral, Why should I commit my weekend to understanding how python dictionary dictionary actually works under the hood, and care about the details linked to open addressing, when I can generate more features far beyond my controls towards a product others could just has well vibe code.

At my most recent workplace, apart from the issues linked with lack of proper management and lack of a concrete vision for the company's moat, I basically clocked out at some point, and just became a feature sprouting organism, attending demos after demos to wow *always almost at the verge of paying clients*. This was sad for me, because outside making a living with the skills i had acquired, I do genuinely love solving problems with the tools in my arsenal.

## What was it really about ?
This probably justifies most people's stance on whether to bother commiting to learning certain technical skills, if it was solely about
implementing a feature, i guess you're better off moving fast and generating one with a LLM model or some agent of sort, but if it was really about mastering systems, understanding principles and unconciously or conciously transferring those skills to other domains then 
the fear of being replaced should be less. This does not invalidate the fear of losing out on a race of doing so much more with limited time and capital resources compared to times before now.

For me, it was always about mastery, I mean that mastery made me work easier, but I always loved the bits and bytes of it all, the quirks you had to get use to and understand why there were, the rationales, the processes, it made commiting to mastery very rewarding All these contributed to buildings ones problem solving muscles, I personal do this thing where I try to do alot of things in a beautifully scheduled way, mimicing async.

## History Repetiting itself
I am sure a certain sect of skilled person experienced things like this in the part, take for example, a painter or an artist, they could have possibly asked themselves, why bother learning to paint and draw when you can get those works done by computers, but still even in the modern world, some of the most priced work of arts are hand made, this doesn't invalidate the craftmanship and the proliferation of the tools for doing so didn't make everyone an artist.

I think this very same argument can be made for what is happening to software engineering and math at this point in history, ofcourse everyone can spin up a React App and get a well documented API powering it, but I believe it's a matter of time, before the hyped dies
and people get bored of the AI slop, is there a place of SI in work, ofcourse, but I have accepted that mastery is personal and it does infact reward more even outside of the technical domain of application.

## Conclusion
Do whatever makes you happy, and if that includes mastery of the internals of Fortran or memorizing factorials of a very large number, by all means go crazy.
