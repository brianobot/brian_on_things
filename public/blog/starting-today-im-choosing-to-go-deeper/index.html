Starting Today, I'm Going Deeper into Game Development with Rust

An academically-minded exploration into game engines, storytelling, and digital universes.

TL;DR
I am documenting my first entry in a series of articles that will come after this one on the topic of game development. I also intend to use this as a Medium to create the sort of learning material I would have appreciated a lot, following through on my game development hobby.

Motivation

Game development has been one of my most desired areas in the software technology sphere, one I have adored with great passion and interest. The ability to create immersive worlds and universes that defined their own rules and had a significant gravitational impact on their fanbase. And most times, it wasn’t just the universes that made people love games, it was the storytelling, the narrative it creates, the characterization, and a lot more in between. This is the part I want to understand and eventually master.

Background

My Journey of tinkering and building software has followed the web development route. From my days of creating web pages with HTML, CSS, and Bootstrap, through my experience building dynamic web pages with Django, I am currently focused on building backend infrastructures for RESTful APIs.

But moving forward, I want to dedicate my free time to researching and creating games, and I want to follow a very academic-inclined methodology, going on deep dives into game development concepts and standards.

“Preparing to do the thing isn’t doing the thing.

Scheduling time to do the thing isn’t doing the thing.

Making a to-do list for the thing isn’t doing the thing.

… Go and do the thing.”—Chris Williamson

So this is me: doing the thing.

Beginning the Work Proper

Game development involves developing the interface and logic of a game. Now there are some toolings out there that make it easy by offering visual tools to customize the different components of your game, but I intend to follow the old way, learning and building every part of my game piece by piece, as was the way of my father before me.

For one, this is a sure way to gain mastery in this topic, but it also ensures that my ability to create worlds is not limited to the tooling at hand, but to my skills with the crude tool (a Programming language). I am sure along my journey, I would have needed to use other toolings for asset creation and management, and when I do, I would dedicate articles to those tools as they apply to game development.

I started working with Rust a while back (Roughly a year now), my confidence in my ability to use its tooling is growing by the day, and I intend to use that as the primary language for my game development and research (I have good expertise with Python Programming, and I intend to use that for scripting when possible). In that same vein, I have decided to go with the Bevy Engine for game development in Rust.

Why Rust

Rust feels like the next logical mutation of my craft; sharp, uncompromising, and strangely elegant. When you’re trying to sculpt worlds where every microsecond can make or break the illusion, safety and performance aren’t luxuries; they’re the foundations of the universe. If I’m going to build digital worlds, I want their limits to be the boundaries of physics, not the fragility of my code..

In games, most times, a lot of things are happening at the same time, or the illusion of a lot of things happening at once is one that the game developer invokes in the minds of the players. Rust's concurrency model offers a sweet support for such a feature, allowing concurrent processing of things to optimize events.

Rust has amazing documentation and support. The Rust community is a very active, religious-like community; there is almost always someone or a reference to help clarify any issues you might be facing with your code logic or program. This understanding creates a motivating mindset and helps new developers get access to support, information, and even tooling to improve their productivity. You definitely do not want to spend days trying to resolve a programming-related issue when you could focus more on your game narrative, story, and world.

Why Bevy

And then there is Bevy, a young Rust-based game engine. I chose Bevy because of the reasons mentioned for Rust, since it is a game engine built in Rust, but it also has some independent perks that make it a choice for my journey

The thought put into Bevy is innovative and beautiful; it is a very data-centric game engine, which is the basis of any world; Data! Allowing game developers to model simple and complex game logics with a simple, systematic approach.

Bevy is also very fast, both in operation and in compile times, which becomes a big deal when making iterative changes to your games during development.

Since this article is written at the start of my journey, I can not speak on the technical sides of things. I have little to no real working experience using Bevy, but from my research, it has proven to be an excellent choice to commit to, and like the saying goes, “You can never know until you try”.

The Road Ahead

Since this is a hobby, I intend to have a systematic and consistent interval of work, documenting my progress in the open, and helping someone like me out there looking for a nice reference for learning game development. I am open to collaboration and corrections to the content and information I put out.

If this sort of thing resonates with you, you should consider subscribing to my articles to get an update when I drop new posts. In the meantime, I would continue to work on my hobby of game development. Until next time. Adios
